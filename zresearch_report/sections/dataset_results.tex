\chapter{Dataset and Experimental Results}
\label{chap:dataset-results}

This chapter describes the construction of the AntRobot natural-language mission dataset, the evaluation protocol used for the language-model stages, and the complete sequence of recorded experiments. The experiments follow the implementation from the initial production-prompt baseline, through few-shot prompting and the unified orchestrated evaluator, to the simplified capability contract and bounded semantic parameter pipeline. Configuration rendering, generated YAML files, and generated launch files are outside the scored boundary; the reported results concern the three language-model stages and their deterministic validation.

\section{Dataset construction}

The dataset was constructed in the direction from a verified structured intent to natural-language expressions. This direction was chosen to keep the reference label independent of the text-generation model. The model generated linguistic variants of an existing annotation; it did not generate the expected capability or parameter answer.

The construction process consisted of the following stages:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fifteen seed missions were defined from capabilities and parameters that exist in the extracted AntRobot system model. Each seed contains a canonical intent, a sparse capability annotation, any explicitly requested parameter values, and descriptive strata.
    \item For each seed, the local \texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b} model generated ten paraphrases. The requested styles included canonical language, natural language, shorthand, imperative phrasing, negative phrasing, and multi-clause requests.
    \item Automatic checks rejected malformed records, duplicate texts after normalization, and records that violated the candidate schema.
    \item A semantic review checked that each paraphrase preserved the seed intent and did not add or remove requirements. Rejected variants were regenerated or replaced. The generation history and rejected texts were retained rather than overwritten.
    \item Fourteen records required a manual language repair after semantic review. The final dataset therefore contains 136 model-generated paraphrases and 14 repaired paraphrases.
    \item The final JSON Lines file was frozen together with generation metadata, raw responses, rejection logs, and repair logs.
\end{enumerate}

The reference annotations are copied from the repository-grounded seed missions. Consequently, the generator cannot improve its evaluation score by placing an answer in the generated text that was not already present in the seed. Nevertheless, the current paraphrase set is marked \texttt{assistant\_reviewed\_pending\_author\_review}. The reported results should therefore be treated as the first experimental baseline and not yet as a final independently verified benchmark result.

\subsection{Dataset contents}

The resulting \texttt{antrobot\_mission\_dataset\_v1.jsonl} file contains 150 missions: 15 intent groups with 10 paraphrases per group. Every record contains a stable identifier, a source-seed identifier, the natural-language mission, the expected capability interpretation, and the expected parameter values. All 150 missions in this version are supported requests. Unsupported and ambiguous requests are therefore not represented in this first benchmark, and safety metrics for those outcomes cannot yet be estimated.

\subsection{Extended train/test datasets}
\label{sec:extended-datasets}

The dataset above is insufficient for measuring rejection and clarification behaviour, since all 150 of its missions are supported requests. Two independently frozen datasets extend the same seed-to-paraphrase construction methodology to cover the full outcome space and to separate development from held-out evaluation: \texttt{antrobot\_train\_mission\_dataset\_v1.jsonl} and \texttt{antrobot\_test\_mission\_dataset\_v1.jsonl}.

Fifty train seeds (\texttt{seed-101}--\texttt{seed-150}) and fifty test seeds (\texttt{seed-151}--\texttt{seed-200}) were authored per split, more than tripling the fifteen-seed pool used above. Each split now spans three outcomes instead of one: \texttt{supported}, \texttt{unsupported}, and \texttt{ambiguous}. Unsupported seeds are drawn from five categories -- sensor, algorithm, odometry, capability, and invalid parameter value -- and ambiguous seeds requiring clarification from five further categories -- missing numeric value, unresolved alternative, qualitative choice, conflicting requirements, and ambiguous reference. Table~\ref{tab:train-test-composition} reports the resulting outcome split; within each split, every category is represented by two or three seeds.

Paraphrase generation for both splits used \texttt{mistral:7b}, rather than \texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b}, which generated the original 150-mission dataset described above. This keeps the model that produces the natural-language surface form distinct from \texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b}, which remains the only model executing and being evaluated on the capability-interpretation and parameter-reasoning stages throughout this chapter: the paraphraser and the reasoner under test are never the same model. Each seed retains three requested styles (canonical, natural, shorthand) in the train split and two (canonical, natural) in the test split, yielding 150 train missions and 100 test missions -- 250 combined, against 150 in the original dataset.

Unlike the original dataset, whose semantic review was completed with fourteen manually repaired records, every paraphrase in both extended splits is recorded as \texttt{pending} review at the time of writing. The expected capability interpretation, parameter values, and outcome categories are still copied directly from the authored seed definitions, so the structured reference labels carry the same provenance guarantee as before; the generated mission text, however, has not yet completed the same manual quality pass. Manual inspection during this work surfaced paraphrases whose wording drifted from the intended operator command -- for example, a configuration request rewritten as a clarification question, or the expected rejection reason restated inside the mission text itself -- which a completed review pass would be expected to catch. Results computed against the extended datasets should be read with this outstanding review step in mind.

\section{Evaluated language-model pipeline}

The evaluation uses the three-stage reasoning pipeline described in Sections~\ref{sec:nl-reasoning} and~\ref{sec:llm-integration}: capability interpretation, parameter selection, and parameter-value reasoning, each a separate call with its own system prompt and structured response schema. All calls in the baseline use \texttt{qwen2.5-coder:7b} with temperature zero. The value-reasoning call is skipped whenever parameter selection returns \texttt{no\_change}, \texttt{unsupported}, or \texttt{needs\_clarification}.

Deterministic capability realization and ROS-system reasoning are executed only to construct the same parameter context used by the production orchestrator. The experiment stops after the third language-model boundary: it does not construct a configuration plan, render YAML, assemble launch files, or score rendered artifacts.

For reproducibility, each prediction stores the exact system prompt, user prompt, raw response, parsed structured object, latency, failure stage, and validation error. The experiment metadata additionally freezes hashes of both datasets, the three prompt templates, capability registry, system model, parameter evidence, configuration schema, and application configuration.

\section{Evaluation metrics}

The metrics distinguish partial semantic understanding from an entirely correct structured prediction.

\subsection{Outcome accuracy}

Outcome accuracy is the proportion of missions assigned the correct terminal status. The possible capability-stage outcomes are \texttt{valid}, \texttt{unsupported}, and \texttt{needs\_clarification}. Since the current benchmark contains only supported missions, this metric measures valid-request acceptance and does not measure unsafe acceptance of unsupported requests.

\subsection{Capability metrics}

Capability exact match requires the complete capability mapping to equal the reference annotation. In the current minimal contract, all four capability keys are present and each value is either an implementation identifier or \texttt{null} for explicit deactivation. Any missing key, extra key, wrong implementation, or incorrect deactivation makes the case incorrect. Capability micro precision and recall instead compare individual capability--value pairs over the full dataset. Their harmonic mean is

\begin{equation}
F_1 = 2 \cdot \frac{P \cdot R}{P + R},
\end{equation}

where $P=TP/(TP+FP)$ and $R=TP/(TP+FN)$. Micro aggregation sums true positives, false positives, and false negatives before computing the score, giving each annotated field equal weight.

\subsection{Parameter-selection metrics}

Parameter-selection exact match requires the predicted parameter-ID set to equal the expected set. Selecting a graph-adjacent but unnecessary parameter, omitting one occurrence of a shared physical parameter, or inventing an identifier all cause exact-match failure. Micro precision, recall, and $F_1$ provide partial credit per parameter identifier across the dataset.

\subsection{Parameter-value metrics}

Parameter-value exact match requires the complete expected identifier--value mapping. Numeric values are evaluated after the annotated unit conversion. The value result is downstream of parameter identification: if the correct identifier is not selected, the value model cannot produce a correct value for it. The present end-to-end value metric therefore measures the combined effect of selection and value reasoning. A later oracle-selection experiment should supply the gold parameter IDs to isolate value-reasoning ability.

\subsection{Joint correctness, completion, robustness, and latency}

Joint LLM exact match requires the correct outcome, an exact capability object, an exact parameter set, and exact values for all applicable stages. Validated inference completion measures whether all required calls returned outputs that passed JSON, schema, bounded-context, identifier, and value validation; completion does not imply semantic correctness.

For the generated dataset, paraphrase consistency is the fraction of seed groups for which all ten variants produce the same structured outputs. Group robustness is stricter: all ten variants must be jointly correct. Mean, median, and 95th-percentile latency describe the cost of all model calls reached by a mission.

\subsection{Step 1: initial production-prompt baseline}

The baseline ran the fixed production prompts over all 155 available records: five diagnostic cases and 150 generated benchmark cases, using the original capability representation, top-12 graph retrieval at one hop, and temperature zero. No evaluation record was used to train or tune the model, so the run is zero-shot with respect to the dataset; the fixed capability prompt nonetheless contains hand-written demonstrations, so it is more precisely a dataset-zero-shot production-prompt baseline than a strict prompt-level zero-shot run.

Capability reasoning showed partial understanding, with 76.1\% micro $F_1$ on the generated set (78.9\% precision, 73.5\% recall), but complete capability objects were correct in only 38.7\% of missions -- many predictions captured most of the requested behaviour while adding or omitting one structured field. Parameter grounding was the dominant failure: only one of 150 generated missions achieved exact parameter selection, and parameter-selection micro $F_1$ was 1.6\%. Frequent errors included invented identifiers and identifiers absent from the bounded retrieval context, which strict validation rejected rather than allowing an ungrounded parameter change; since value reasoning depends on the selected identifiers, parameter-value scores were also 1.6\% micro $F_1$. This does not establish that the third call cannot reason about values, only that the selection-to-value path rarely supplied it with a valid, correct selection. No mission satisfied every strict LLM criterion simultaneously, so joint exact match was zero -- a result that should not be read as uniformly absent language understanding, given the substantially higher capability micro $F_1$, but as evidence that partial capability correctness is insufficient once the downstream parameter contract fails. Only 43.3\% of missions completed all applicable validated calls: 85 generated-set missions produced validation exceptions, 82 at parameter reasoning and three at capability interpretation, reinforcing that the parameter boundary dominates the first baseline's failures rather than model-server availability or rendering. Only one of the 15 intent groups produced identical structured outputs for all ten paraphrases (6.7\% paraphrase consistency), and no group had all ten paraphrases jointly correct.

\subsection{Step 2: few-shot prompt demonstrations}

The few-shot run held the model, temperature, dataset, retrieval settings, graph depth, schemas, and deterministic context construction fixed and replaced the three system prompts with systematic demonstrations. The demonstrations used novel wording and values rather than copying benchmark records, and represented capability-only, parameter-only, and combined capability--parameter requests. The parameter-selection prompt specifically demonstrated that capability-only missions should return \texttt{no\_change}, that selected identifiers must occur in the bounded context, and that two consumers of one physical wheel property must be selected together; the value prompt demonstrated unit conversion, consistent coupled values, and copying the current value into \texttt{old\_value}.

Outcome accuracy increased from 63.3\% to 90.7\%, and parameter-selection and parameter-value micro $F_1$ each increased from 1.6\% to 11.8\%, indicating that more individual identifiers and values were recovered. However, exact parameter accuracy did not improve, capability micro $F_1$ decreased by 4.9 points, and no mission achieved joint exact correctness. Validated completion fell from 43.3\% to 18.0\%, with 116 parameter-reasoning exceptions and seven capability-interpretation exceptions: the demonstrations improved partial semantic recognition but also encouraged the model to attempt more downstream reasoning that failed strict schema or grounding checks. This motivated structural separation and deterministic context control rather than relying on examples alone; the few-shot intervention identifies the parameter prompt and constrained decoding behaviour as targets for controlled refinement rather than resolving them by itself.

\subsection{Step 3: unified orchestrated evaluation}

The unified run evaluated the three agents in the same order and with the same intermediate contracts used by the application. Parameter retrieval was reduced to ten candidates and enriched with LLM-derived aliases, user expressions, behavioral descriptions, constraints, and typed relationships. Retrieval ranks and scores were kept outside the model-facing context; capability realization restricted the catalogue to active components; capability-only requests were prevented from becoming invented launch-argument changes; and selection and value generation were separated. A single validation-guided retry was internal to the pipeline. These changes raised capability exact match to 75.3\%, parameter-selection exact match to 53.3\%, joint exact match to 40.0\%, and validated completion to 94.7\%. The run demonstrated successful composition of the independently tested agents, but capability errors still propagated into retrieval and parameter reasoning.

\subsection{Step 4: minimal capability contract}

The final recorded experiment simplified capability output to four direct capability values. Each value is the selected implementation, while \texttt{null} is reserved for an explicitly disabled capability. The capability prompt preserves registry defaults for unmentioned capabilities, changes an implementation only when requested, distinguishes capability selection from parameter tuning, and omits inference provenance such as \texttt{selection\_basis}. Dataset annotations and deterministic realization were updated to the same contract. Additional prompt rules addressed negation and short parameter-only missions, while active-component filtering, semantic top-10 retrieval, bounded parameter selection, value-only reasoning, and deterministic validation remained in place.

This final step produced the strongest result in every semantic correctness measure: capability exact match reached 96.7\%, parameter-selection and parameter-value exact match reached 88.7\%, and 132 of 150 missions were jointly exact, corresponding to 88.0\%. Only two missions ended in runtime or validation errors, and 98.7\% completed the validated pipeline. Nine of the fifteen seed groups were correct for all ten paraphrases. Mean latency increased to 8.56 seconds because more missions successfully reached all applicable reasoning stages and the internal correction path could be exercised.

For parameter-changing missions specifically, the minimal-contract artifact reports retrieval recall of 65.8\% at rank one, 98.7\% within the top three, and 100\% within the top five. Selection exact-set accuracy over the 80 parameter-change cases was 83.8\%, individual-value accuracy was 96.4\%, and complete-value-set accuracy was 83.8\%. This supports the staged design: once the correct bounded identifiers are available, generating their values is considerably more reliable than identifying the complete parameter set.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:dataset-results-limitations}

Each artifact in Table~\ref{tab:experiment-progression} is a single run of one quantized local model. Temperature zero improves repeatability but does not guarantee identical outputs across inference-runtime versions or hardware, so repeated runs would be needed for variance estimates and confidence intervals, which are not yet available. The four-column progression also combines prompt, contract, dataset-label, retrieval, and orchestration changes rather than isolating one factor at a time: it demonstrates the improvement of the implemented system, but controlled ablations are required to quantify the contribution of each intervention independently.

The 150-mission dataset used throughout this progression still requires final independent author review, and its all-supported composition prevents measurement of unsupported-request rejection, clarification recall, or unsafe execution. The extended train/test datasets introduced in Section~\ref{sec:extended-datasets} begin to address this gap, but are themselves still pending the same review step. These limitations are turned into concrete next activities in Section~\ref{sec:next-activities}.
